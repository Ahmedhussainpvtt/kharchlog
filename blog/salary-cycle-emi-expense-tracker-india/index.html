Payday to payday: EMI-aware Expense tracker for salaried India

2026-09-17

Month-start EMI, rent, and SIPs land before the next salary. Use an Expense tracker aligned to your salary cycle, not a calendar month that hides the squeeze.

Calendar months lie. If salary hits on the 1st and EMIs clear on the 5th, a 31-day report still looks “fine” while the 6th to 25th is tight. An Expense tracker should follow your payday, not only January-December labels.

Map the real cycle

  1. Write your salary credit date (or typical window).
  2. List EMIs, rent, insurance, and SIPs with debit dates.
  3. Treat that window as one “money month” in the Sheet.

What to watch after payday

  • Fixed outflows first: EMI, rent, school fees
  • Then groceries and UPI food
  • Then discretionary shopping and OTT

If food UPI spikes in week two, you still have time. That is the same idea as mid-month leak checks, aimed at salaried cashflow.

EMI rows vs “mystery debits”

Loan SMS often names the lender, not “EMI.” In Kharch Log, pin a payee once (HDFC Bank EMI, Bajaj, etc.) so the next SMS inherits it. The Expense tracker then shows EMI as a line, not noise.

Kharch Log + your Sheet

SMS drafts land on Android; the durable book is your Google Sheet. Filter the payday-to-payday range, hide reimbursements, and export if a lender asks for spends. Related: EMI tracking, reports.
