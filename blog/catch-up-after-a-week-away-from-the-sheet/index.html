Catch up after a week away from the Sheet

2026-10-07

SMS kept arriving while you were away. Sort that week by amount in your Google Sheet, fix the large rows, drop duplicates, add cash you remember, and stop.

A week away does not pause your bank. Hotels, reloads, refunds, and ordinary subscriptions still send SMS. Scrolling that week in time order is how you get tired and label nothing. You need a catch-up that actually ends. The alerts can already be in your Google Sheet. Kharch Log stores them from the phone SMS, with no bank login. You are not rebuilding the week from several apps. Sort by amount, fix the large rows, drop duplicates, and add the cash you really remember. Then stop.

Sort the gap by amount

Filter the days you were away and sort the amounts from high to low. Label the hotel, ticket, rent, or bill before any small row. A trip name or a rent month takes seconds on a large debit. Do not start with the smallest UPI. Starting small is how you quit before the row that changes the month. Keep the sitting to half an hour. Name the large money. Leave perfection.

Before you panic, label transfers, SIPs, and card bill payments for what they are. Catch-up panic treats every debit as lifestyle. A SIP is not the trip. A card bill payment is not a new shopping spree. After those are parked, the remaining large rows are the week. Write one header sentence about what the week was. That stops the next Sunday from reopening the whole pile. Do this before the small rows.

  • Filter the dates you missed and sort amount high to low
  • Label rent, travel, and bills before small UPI
  • Park SIPs, self-transfers, and card bill payments first
  • Cap the sitting at about half an hour

Drop duplicate alerts for one debit

One UPI can create two alerts, from the bank and from the app. After a week away you can double a hotel if you miss the pair. Sort by amount and look for twins close in time. Keep the bank debit. Mark the other as a duplicate so it stays out of the total. Amount, time, and a piece of the payee are enough to decide. Both messages may be stored because both arrived. The total should count the payment once.

Match refunds that arrived while you were away, or the trip looks too expensive. A reversal is not income. Link it to the debit. Scheduled renewals still need their usual category. They are not a blank-row emergency. Label them quickly and move on. You are finishing the week, not auditing every subscription today. A deeper subscription pass can be another day. Leave their amounts as the SMS sent them. Do not reopen the plan on a catch-up night.

Add only the cash you can picture

Add only the cash you can picture: a fare, a gift, a meal in notes. Use one approximate row for the vague rest, or skip it. Do not invent a diary. ATM rows in that week still follow the method in your header. Under the transfer method they are not extra spend. Under the rough method they are the spend, so do not add the meals as well. Being away does not justify a third method.

A transfer home from a hotel is still family, not travel. Put the month in the note. A client credit that landed on the road is still business in. Catch-up is when labels get sloppy. Sloppy labels make the next monthly close harder. Use the categories you already trust. If you do not know a payee, leave it blank and finish the ones you know. One unknown is better than a confident wrong story.

  • Specific cash only, or one row marked approximate
  • Do not itemise snacks if the withdrawal was already the spend
  • Family transfers stay family even if you sent them while travelling
  • An unknown mid-sized payee can wait a day

Stop while the week is usable

Stop when large rows are labelled, duplicates are marked, and remembered cash is typed. If the leftover blanks are small, note their total and leave them. Do not invent categories for forty tiny payees at midnight. A short pass the next day can finish them, or the weekly review can. Catch-up should make the week usable. It does not need an empty queue in one night. Unlabelled SMS are still in the Sheet. They are waiting, not lost.

Then look at one number: the trip sum, or the week's spending if you were at home. If it is fine, stop for the night. If it is not, change one thing in the coming week. Write that single change. Five new rules after a messy catch-up usually means none of them stick. The Sheet held the SMS until you returned. You labelled the big rows and chose one change. The following week can return to a shorter daily habit.

Questions people actually ask

I missed two weeks. Do I change the steps? Use the same steps. Do the large rows in one sitting, then a second short sitting if you need it. Do not clear every tiny row at once. Sort the whole gap by amount so a repeated charge is obvious. Keep a trip name if the travel spans the gap. Approximate the cash once, then go back to daily capture.

Can I skip the missed week and start fresh? No. Hiding the week makes the next comparison false, and the money is still gone. You do not need perfect categories. You need large amounts in the right buckets and duplicates removed. A blank week hides rent or a hotel inside an unlabelled pile. A short sitting beats a brand new Sheet. The rows are already dated.

I cannot remember the cash at all. What do I type? Do not invent a cash diary. For that gap only, you can treat ATM withdrawals as the cash spend and say so. Do not also guess snacks. Return to your normal method the next day. A labelled rough week is honest. A made-up list is not. If there was no withdrawal and no memory, leave cash blank.
