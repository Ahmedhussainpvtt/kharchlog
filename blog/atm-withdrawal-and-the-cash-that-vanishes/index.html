ATM cash and the notes that vanish without an SMS

2026-10-03

An ATM withdrawal moves money into your pocket. Log the cash spends or the withdrawal, not both, so the month in your Google Sheet is not doubled.

An ATM SMS feels like an expense because the balance drops. It is your own money moving into notes. Those notes pay for autos, tea, or a shop that will never message you. If you label the withdrawal as spending and also type the cash purchases, you double the month. If you label the withdrawal and type nothing else, you have a number with no story. Kharch Log sees the withdrawal SMS and can place it in your Google Sheet. There is no bank login. Pick one method and keep it.

Choose transfer-to-cash or one rough total

Label the ATM debit as a transfer to cash, not as food or misc. Spending happens when the notes leave your pocket. Type the cash spends you care about the same day: amount, category, and place. Those rows are the spending. The withdrawal should be close to cash rows plus what is still in your wallet. If the wallet is empty and the rows are much smaller, the gap is the leak. This is accurate, and it needs a same-day habit. The app cannot invent a cash row the bank never sent.

If you will not type daily cash, label the whole withdrawal as cash spend that day. Do not also log the tea and the auto. You lose the split. You keep a true total. You may add a rough note, and you should not treat a guess as exact. Do not switch methods in the middle of the month. Using both makes you look like you spent the cash twice. Choose on the 1st and keep it for the month.

  • Accurate: ATM is a transfer, each cash spend is a row
  • Rough: the withdrawal is the spend, and you do not log the notes again
  • Never both in the same month
  • Write the method in the header on the day you choose it

Type cash the same night, or do not split it

If you type cash spends, do it the same night. An auto, a stall, and a chemist can be three categories in one evening. Waiting until Sunday pushes them into misc. Three lines on the phone, then into the Sheet before the next afternoon. Each spend keeps its own category. Do not paste them all onto the ATM row as one blob. A blob is the rough method. A blob plus detailed rows is the double count.

Several withdrawals in a week are refills of the same pocket, not several lifestyles. In the transfer method, none of them are spending. In the rough method, unused cash overstates the month until you adjust. A deposit of leftover notes is a transfer back, not income. Match it to the withdrawal. Labelling that deposit as salary makes income disagree with your real pay. Both SMS will be in the Sheet. The labels stop the leftover cash from looking like a bonus.

A missed week needs one honest estimate

If a week has no cash rows, do not reconstruct every cup of tea. Add one approximate row for the categories you are sure about, marked approximate. Then go back to same-day typing. A large cash item, such as a repair or a gift, should be typed on its own. Leave the small leftovers in the estimate. The large item is what should change a decision. Say approximate in the note so you do not treat it as a receipt later.

At month end, compare withdrawals with the cash rows if you use the accurate method. A widening gap means the typing slipped, not that the bank SMS is wrong. Do not edit the withdrawal amount to match your memory. The SMS figure is what left the account. If you use the rough method, skip this comparison. The withdrawal total is already the cash spend. Write the method in the header on the day you choose it.

  • Do not rebuild every small cash purchase from memory
  • Type large cash items exactly, and mark the rest approximate
  • Never edit the ATM amount to force a match
  • A widening gap means the habit slipped

One household, one cash rule

Cash you give someone for daily use is either a transfer or an allowance. If they will log the spends, your side is only a transfer. If the money is an allowance you will not track, categorise it and stop. If both of you log the snacks, you double them. The usual failure is two people using two methods on the same notes. Agree once. A shared Sheet shows the clash when both rows exist. Cash will not send a second SMS to reconcile you.

Cash withdrawn through a UPI app is still a withdrawal. Treat it like the ATM. The SMS may name the app. The rule does not change. For a trip, you can log the converted cash as one travel bundle. That is the rough method for that bundle only. Note the trip name. Do not mix it into the home method without a header sentence. One labelled exception is fine. A silent exception makes the next month's cash total hard to trust.

Questions people actually ask

Is the ATM fee itself an expense? Yes. The bank fee is a fee, not part of the notes you walked away with. If the SMS separates them, log the fee as fees and the cash under your usual method. If one SMS combines them, note the fee or split it in the note. A repeated ATM charge is worth seeing. It is not tea, and it should not hide inside misc.

I withdrew cash for a wedding gift. How do I label it? Under the transfer method, the ATM stays a transfer and the envelope is a gift row. Under the rough method, do not add a second gift row. Split the withdrawal note into gift and other cash that add to the SMS. A named gift is useful later. An ATM row left as misc is not. Match the method already written in your header.

What if no ATM SMS arrives? Add the withdrawal from the receipt the same day, and mark the row manual. Use the method you already chose. Do not skip it because no SMS arrived. Missing cash-in with typed spends makes spending appear from nowhere. Missing cash-in under the rough method hides the spend entirely. When there is no SMS, typing the row is the record.
